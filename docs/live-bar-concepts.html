Live × timeline — four ways one bar gets built

The Live view shows blocks landing as responses stream in; the Graph view shows the current hour's bar growing, then settling into history. Each mockup below fuses the two: the rightmost slot is alive, being built out of the live feed, and when the hour rolls over it hardens into a normal bar and slides onto the shelf. Time is compressed — each "hour" lasts nine seconds here.

hour rolls in 9s

01Masonry

Every response is a brick dropped onto the hour's pile — at the tick, the rubble sorts itself into a clean stacked bar.

Why it's satisfying: the messy pile visibly becomes the tidy bar — same pixels, rearranged. The most literal merge of the two current views.

02Hourglass

Tokens pour as sand, grain by grain, heaping up in the current slot — the tick compresses the dune into a solid bar.

Why it's satisfying: pour-and-pile is primally pleasing, and big responses read as a heavier stream. The heap's rough top compacting flat is the "cement" moment.

03The glass

The current hour is a glass filling with model-colored liquid; each response sloshes in. At the tick it sets like resin and slides out.

Why it's satisfying: the layers record the order your afternoon actually happened in, then re-sort into model bands as the liquid cures — a tiny story every hour. (It's the Live tab's "cup," grown up.)

04Molten edge

A plain stacked bar, but the live one is being forged: fresh tokens land white-hot and cool into their model's color.

Why it's satisfying: the heat gradient is the recency signal — you can see the last 30 seconds cooling in place. Cheapest to build in WPF: it's the existing chart plus a glow and a color tween.

Reading the field: 01 keeps the block identity from the Live view all the way into history. 02 is the most hypnotic to leave on a second monitor. 03 preserves sequence — the one thing the stacked bar erases. 04 is the pragmatic pick: same chart the Graph tab already draws, with a forge-glow that makes "this bar is now" unmistakable.
