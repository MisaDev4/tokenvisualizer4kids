# Token Town — the aesthetic charter

Binding on every agent that touches the town. If a change would look wrong in an
old-fashioned village, it is wrong, however clever it is.

## The place we are building

A believable pre-industrial **village** — thatch and tile, timber and plaster, dirt lanes
worn into cobbles, a mill by the water, smoke from chimneys, people out in the street. It
grows denser and more established as the day's tokens climb; it never becomes a modern city
and it never becomes a fantasy realm.

The user's words: *"I think it should still keep the old fashioned style, like, village
aesthetic"* and *"more realistic"*.

### The three rules

1. **Growth shows as MORE, not TALLER.** A bigger day means more cottages, more lanes, more
   villagers, more life — not a taller monument or a grander wizard tower. Density and
   activity are the reward.
2. **The people are the point.** Population is what the user should notice first when their
   number goes up. Anything that competes with the crowd for attention is suspect.
3. **Nothing magical.** No arcane towers, no floating geometry, no sky-gates, no glowing
   sci-fi. Gold leaf on a weather vane is fine; a levitating orb is not.

## The centrepiece is small

The monument is being cut down to **a village fountain with the token count above it**
(idea T-19). It is a fountain in a market square, not a cathedral of the coin. It may gain
modest dressing as the town grows — better stonework, a carved basin, flowers, lanterns —
but it must never again dominate the skyline or read as the town's reason for existing.

## Landmarks: retired and replaced

The fantasy-grand set fights the village. **Retire** `wizard_tower`, `skygate`,
`observatory`, `golden_statue`, and rustic-ify `cathedral` into a modest parish **chapel**.

Replace them with landmarks a real village would actually earn, in roughly this order as
the town grows:

| keep / new | what it is |
|---|---|
| `well` | the village well — the first thing a settlement gets |
| `smithy` | forge, anvil, glowing coals at night, hammer sounds |
| `mill` (was `windmill`) | wind or water depending on the plan's archetype |
| `market_cross` | the stone cross at the centre of a market village |
| `tithe_barn` | the big timber barn — a sign of a prosperous parish |
| `market_hall` | timber-framed, open ground floor |
| `chapel` (was `cathedral`) | small parish church, squat tower, NOT a cathedral |
| `bell_tower` | keep — a village bell is right, at village scale |
| `village_green` | a proper green with a pond and geese |
| `inn` | the coaching inn, lit windows, a yard, the busiest building in town |

Landmark heights come DOWN. Nothing should tower: a chapel tower is the tallest thing in a
village and it is not very tall. `hTop` above ~14 needs a good reason.

## Materials and light: aim for real, not stylised

- Thatch, clay tile, slate, lime plaster, oak timber, fieldstone, brick. Weathering,
  staining under eaves, moss on north faces, mud at gateways.
- Roofs are the biggest surface the camera sees — they carry the most detail budget.
- Warm interior light spilling from small windows at night; lanterns, braziers, the forge.
- Avoid saturated primaries and plastic-looking uniform surfaces. Variation comes from
  weathering and material, not from hue.

## Village tiers (idea T-21)

Milestones upgrade the *place*, visibly, without changing what kind of place it is:
hamlet → village → market village → market town. Show it through lanes becoming cobbled,
thatch giving way to tile, fences becoming walls and hedges, a market square filling in,
more lanterns, more traffic, more smoke — and through the landmark ladder above.

## What to do when in doubt

Ask: *would a photograph of a real village look like this?* If not, change it.
